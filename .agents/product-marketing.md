## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Fajr Now is a Fajr-centered morning system for Muslims.

**What it does:** It helps a user understand what tomorrow morning requires, resolve an appropriate wake plan around Fajr and relevant context, execute that plan reliably within platform constraints, and move through the morning with minimal friction.

**Category:** Fajr-centered morning / wake-planning product.

**Product type:** Consumer mobile product with a supporting website. Exact commercial model is not yet confirmed in this repository.

## Target Audience
**Primary audience:** Practicing Muslims who want their mornings reliably organized around Fajr.

**Secondary contexts:** Shift workers, travelers, students, parents, new Muslims, and users whose schedules or observance patterns vary.

**Primary use case:** Understand and execute tomorrow morning around Fajr without repetitive calculation or generic alarm setup.

**Jobs to be done:**
- Know what tomorrow morning looks like.
- Know when and how to wake for that morning.
- Reduce repeated setup and mental calculation.
- Trust that the plan reflects current settings, location, time conditions, and relevant context.
- Recover from inconsistency without shame or unnecessary complexity.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Confirmed from product doctrine:**
- Generic alarm clocks begin with arbitrary time entry rather than the meaning of the morning.
- Fajr varies with date, location, calculation settings, daylight-saving/timezone changes, and user context.
- Repetitive alarm configuration and mental calculation create friction.
- Alarm reliability can be weakened by platform permissions and operating-system constraints.
- Broad Islamic apps and generic wake-enforcement apps solve adjacent problems but do not own the whole Muslim morning-resolution problem.

**Research status:** These are product hypotheses/doctrine until validated with customer evidence. Do not present them as statistically established customer findings.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct/adjacent categories:**
- Generic clock/alarm apps — strong at arbitrary time entry, weak at resolving the Muslim morning.
- Wake-enforcement apps — strong at alarm friction, not built around Fajr or the morning as a resolved system.
- Sleep apps — optimize sleep timing rather than the Fajr-centered morning.
- Broad Muslim apps — offer breadth, but the product strategy here intentionally focuses on the morning.

Specific competitor claims require current research before publication.

## Differentiation
- Anchor-first rather than time-entry-first.
- One morning engine that resolves multiple contexts rather than separate feature silos.
- Explain what tomorrow requires before asking for configuration.
- Reliability, privacy, clarity, and predictable behavior are product features, not marketing afterthoughts.
- Purposefully narrow focus on the Muslim morning rather than broad engagement.

## Objections / Questions to Validate
- Why is this better than setting a normal phone alarm?
- How reliable can it actually be given iOS/Android restrictions?
- Does it calculate Fajr in a way that matches my preferred method?
- What data/location access does it require?
- Will it become another noisy religious/productivity app?
- Does it work when traveling, changing timezones, or on unusual schedules?

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Repetitive setup, generic alarms, uncertainty about tomorrow's timing, fragmented tools.

**Pull:** A morning already resolved around Fajr, less mental setup, clearer execution, calm purpose-built experience.

**Habit:** Existing phone alarms are familiar, free, and already installed.

**Anxiety:** Trust in prayer-time calculations, alarm delivery, permissions, privacy, complexity, and whether the product overreaches religiously.
